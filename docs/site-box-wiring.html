Wiring the Site A box

Order of work, with a meter

  1. Lay it out dry before sticking anything: PSU along the back with air over its fan, fuse box and Wagos near where the runs leave, Pi and amp where their leads reach. If the floor is too tight, the end walls work: the box is 140mm deep.
  2. Mains, unplugged. Wires 1–3, then the cover. With a meter, earth pin of the plug to the PSU case: under 1Ω. L and N to case: open circuit. Input switch on 230V.
  3. First power-up, no fuses in. Plug into the RCD socket. PSU +V to −V: 12.0V (trim with ADJ to 12.0–12.3V if needed). Unplug.
  4. 12V side. Wires 4–7. Fit F7 only, power up, check the USB buck's port reads 5.0–5.25V (meter on the outer two pins of the USB-A socket). Unplug.
  5. Pi. Wires 16–19. Power up. Once FPP has booted, vcgencmd get_throttled should say 0x0 (no under-voltage).
  6. Amp. Wires 20–24, volume pot halfway, fit F8. Play a test track from FPP: left and right in the right speakers, no hum or crackle. The amp shouldn't be hot after 10 minutes.
  7. Trees, one at a time. Make up the run, fit its fuse, and at the SP17 at the tree end read +12V with red on the + pin, black on the other. Then plug it into the tree. Do the next.
  8. Tidy and close. Tie every cable down (below), check each lid seal grips its cable, then clamp the lid.

The mains side is the only part that can hurt you. If you'd like a second pair of eyes on wires 1–3 and the cover, that's the bit to have checked. Never work in the box with the plug in.

Sticking it down

  • Test the pads first. Weatherproof boxes are often polypropylene, which ordinary foam pads don't grip. Stick one pad and one tie mount to the floor overnight and try to pull them off. If they lift, use 3M VHB, or pads sold for low-surface-energy plastics.
  • Stick indoors, warm. Wipe both faces with isopropyl, press hard for 30 seconds, and leave it 24 hours before loading. Adhesive put on cold, outside in November, barely grabs.
  • Don't trust pads alone for the PSU. It weighs about a kilo and has a fan. Stick it, then strap it down with a cable tie through a tie mount on each side, or two.
  • Leave the fan face clear by 20mm or more.
  • Pi: stick its case down, not the bare board, so you can lift it out to get at the microSD.
  • Amp, sound card and the Pi's USB buck go in two printed pods (enclosure/player-pods.FCStd): each board sits on a foam pad inside its tray, the clip-on lid holds it down (push the hooks in through the side windows to open it), and the pod's end flanges take cable ties. Stick the pods down, not the boards.
  • Fuse box: big pads or VHB. Pushing a fuse in takes real force.
  • Tie mounts: tie every cable down 30–50mm inside the lid seal, so a tug on a run pulls on the tie, not on a crimp or a Wago.
  • The mains lead matters most. Two tie mounts, ties pulled tight on the sheath, then give it a firm tug. If it moves at all, it needs a proper clamp before it goes outside.
  • Drip loops: outside the box, let every cable dip below the seal before it rises, so water drips off instead of running in.
